title: "Procedural Content Tables: Six Pools, 25 Entries Each"
sidebarTitle: "Procedural Tables"
description: "Terminal 1d6 RPG uses six procedural content pools — bosses, henchmen, locations, loot, good events, and bad events — each with 25 unique entries."
---

The world of Terminal 1d6 RPG is assembled from six pools of 25 entries each — bosses, henchmen, locations, loot, good events, and bad events. Every time the game draws from a pool, it rolls a 1d6 to select an index within the remaining entries, then permanently removes that entry from the pool for the rest of your run. This means no two encounters, locations, or windfalls repeat within a single campaign, and the shape of your adventure shifts with every playthrough.

## Bosses (25 entries)

Bosses are powerful named adversaries who anchor major encounters. Each boss has a distinct identity — a title, a lineage, or a curse — that the Dungeon Master weaves into the encounter narrative. You face one boss per run until the pool is exhausted.

Sample entries:

- Malakar the Void-Eyed Lich
- General Vane of the Iron Cinder
- Aurelia the Weeping Gorgon
- The Fractured Clockwork Monarch
- Ignis Rex the Ash Sovereign
- Seraphel the Hollow Prophet

## Henchmen (25 entries)

Henchmen are lesser foes — scouts, cultists, undead sentinels, and wandering predators — who test your mettle between boss encounters. They hit hard enough to matter but fall faster than a true boss, making each skirmish a resource decision rather than a guaranteed victory.

Sample entries:

- Rusted-blade Goblin Skulker
- Cursed Cultist of the Moon
- Shadow-Bound Rogue
- Skeletal Crossbowman
- Frost-Bitten Wolf-Scout
- Plague-Touched Swamp Wraith

## Locations (25 entries)

Every encounter happens somewhere. The location pool provides the dungeon's atmosphere — flooded crypts, shattered citadels, spore-choked forests — and the Dungeon Master sets the scene using whichever location is drawn for that stage of your journey.

Sample entries:

- Sunken Crypt of the Weeping Martyr
- The Shattered Obsidian Citadel
- Drowned Necropolis of Val-Kesh
- The Twilight Spore Forest
- The Hall of the Crimson Eclipse
- Ember Wastes of the Forsaken March

## Loot (25 entries)

Treasure comes in the form of named magical items — rings, blades, cloaks, elixirs, and talismans — each with a flavourful identity. When you claim loot, the item is drawn from this pool and added to your character sheet, never to appear again in the same run.

Sample entries:

- Gilded Ring of Whispering Phantoms
- Amulet of the Ashen Phoenix
- Runed Dagger of Soul-Siphon
- Cloak of the Umbral Weave
- Elixir of Bottled Starlight
- Talisman of the Unbroken Chain

## Good Events (25 entries)

When you roll a **6**, a triumph narrative fires from the good events pool. These short, vivid passages describe the moment fortune turns decisively in your favour — a perfect parry, a tactical blunder from your enemy, or a sudden surge of power.

Sample entries:

- "You exploit a tactical blunder, staggering the foe with absolute force."
- "A miraculous deflection sends the attacker's weapon clattering away."
- "An opening appears in the enemy's stance — you seize it without hesitation."
- "Fortune bends the arc of their strike wide; your counter lands clean and true."
- "The ground shifts in your favour, and the enemy stumbles into your blade."
- "A sudden surge of will steadies your arm — the blow is perfect."

## Bad Events (25 entries)

When you roll a **1**, the bad events pool delivers a misfortune narrative — an unwelcome twist that reminds you how thin the margin between survival and disaster truly is. Each bad event also costs you **1 HP**, and reaching 0 HP ends your run.

Sample entries:

- "An enemy blade cuts through your guard, opening a bleeding gash."
- "A toxic miasma triggers a fit of racking, agonizing coughs."
- "Your footing betrays you; the enemy's strike connects before you can recover."
- "A sudden cramp seizes your sword arm, leaving you dangerously exposed."
- "The shadows conspire — a blow lands from an angle you never saw coming."
- "Your weapon snags on debris; the enemy takes full advantage of your stumble."

## How Drawing Works

Each draw follows the same three-step process:

1. The game rolls a 1d6 to determine an offset index within the current pool.
2. The entry at that index is selected and returned to the Dungeon Master.
3. The entry is immediately removed from the pool so it cannot be drawn again.

Because the index is chosen by a live die roll rather than sequentially, you cannot predict which entry comes next — even if you know how many remain.

<Info>
  When a pool is fully exhausted, the game returns the message: "The void stares back; the pool is dry."
</Info>

<Tip>
  Starting loot and the starting henchman are also drawn from these tables, so your very first encounter may use one of the best items.
</Tip>
