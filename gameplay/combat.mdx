Combat in Terminal 1d6 RPG is entirely freeform. You describe what your character does in plain text — a lunge, a feint, a desperate spell — and the 1d6 handles the rest. There are no attack rolls separate from defense rolls, no action economy, and no turn order to manage. Every input you type is one action, one roll, one outcome.

## Starting Combat

You never have to seek out a fight. The moment your Character Chronicle is confirmed, the game places you directly in front of your first enemy. The terminal displays your starting location, names the henchman standing between you and the dungeon ahead, and shows the loot item at stake. From that point forward, every line you type is your next move.

## Types of Actions

The game accepts any free-text input — you are not limited to a command list. That said, most actions fall into one of these categories:

- **Attack** — describe a direct offensive move. Examples: *Strike with my sword*, *Cast a fireball at its chest*, *Drive my dagger into the gap in its armor*.
- **Defend / Evade** — describe evasion or protection. Examples: *Dodge behind a pillar*, *Raise my shield and brace*, *Step back and let the blow glance off my pauldron*.
- **Trick / Use Environment** — describe exploiting your surroundings. Examples: *Knock over the torch sconce to start a fire*, *Throw sand from the floor into its eyes*, *Collapse the rotted balcony onto the enemy below*.
- **Flee / Retreat** — describe disengaging or escaping the encounter. The die still rolls — fleeing is not automatically safe.

The Dungeon Master responds to exactly what you write. The more specific your description, the more textured the narration in return.

## Enemy Types

Every enemy the game generates is one of two tiers:

- **Henchmen** — weaker foes that populate most encounters. They appear from the start of the campaign and are generated frequently during Encounter Shifts. Examples: *Rusted-blade Goblin Skulker*, *Shadow-Bound Rogue*, *Plague-Marked Crossbowman of the Ashen Keep*.
- **Bosses** — powerful adversaries that appear when the internal encounter roll lands on 5 or 6 during a shift. Bosses receive more elaborate DM narration and their presence signals a high-stakes moment in the story. Examples: *Malakar the Void-Eyed Lich*, *General Vane of the Iron Cinder*.

## Tracking Your HP

Your current HP is always visible in the **top-left badge** of the header, updated in real time after every relevant roll. The badge changes color as your health drops:

| HP Range | Badge Color | Status |
|----------|-------------|--------|
| 7 – 10 HP | 🟢 Green | Healthy |
| 4 – 6 HP | 🟡 Yellow | Caution |
| 1 – 3 HP | 🔴 Red (flashing) | Critical |

Only a roll of 1 reduces your HP — each Dire Misfortune outcome costs exactly 1 HP. There is no way to restore health once lost.

## Death

When your HP reaches **0**, the game halts combat and generates a death epilogue — a final passage of narration describing the last moments of your hero. The campaign ends there. From the death screen you can choose to **Export Story** (download the full transcript of your run) or **Reset** to begin a new campaign with a fresh character.

<Note>
  There is no healing mechanic in Terminal 1d6. HP only decreases over the course of a campaign. Your survival depends entirely on the die — keep that in mind every time you engage rather than evade.
</Note>

<Tip>
  Even non-combat actions — exploring a room, sneaking past a guard, attempting to reason with an enemy — still roll the die. A 1 on any of those inputs costs you 1 HP just as surely as a failed sword swing. No action is truly safe.
</Tip>
