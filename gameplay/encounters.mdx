After every successful action — any roll of 4, 5, or 6 — the game checks whether your current encounter should advance. When it does, the story pulls you forward into a new location, places a new enemy in front of you, and signals the shift in the terminal with a distinctive purple message. This is how you explore the world of Terminal 1d6: not through a map or menu, but through the momentum of good rolls.

## When Encounter Shifts Happen

The trigger conditions depend on what you rolled:

- **Roll of 4 or 5 (Positive Progress):** A secondary internal d6 rolls immediately after your outcome is narrated. If that internal roll lands on **4 or higher**, the encounter shifts. Rolls of 1–3 on the internal die keep you in your current encounter.
- **Roll of 6 (Perfect Triumph):** An encounter shift always happens — no secondary roll is needed. Every Perfect Triumph advances the story.
- **Rolls of 1, 2, or 3:** No encounter shift occurs. A roll of 1 triggers a calamity event but keeps you in place. Rolls of 2 and 3 leave you holding ground in your current encounter.

## What Happens During a Shift

When an encounter shift fires, three things happen simultaneously:

1. **A new location is drawn** from the 25-entry location pool. Each location has a unique name and atmosphere that the Dungeon Master uses to frame the scene. Examples from the pool include *The Shattered Obsidian Citadel* and *Drowned Necropolis of Val-Kesh*.
2. **A new enemy appears** — the game rolls an internal d6 to determine the tier. A result of **1–4** produces a **henchman**; a result of **5–6** produces a **boss**. Each enemy is drawn from its own named pool and removed after use.
3. **The encounter message prints in purple** in the terminal, visually separating the shift from ordinary narration so you always know when you've moved forward.

## Pool Depletion

Every location, enemy, and loot item drawn during your campaign is removed from its pool and does not repeat. Each pool holds **25 entries**. As your run extends, the pools grow thinner. Once all 25 entries in a given pool have been drawn, any further draw from that pool returns:

```
The void stares back; the pool is dry.
```

This is a hard boundary on each campaign's content — not a bug. Terminal 1d6 is designed around a finite universe of 25 locations, 25 enemies, and 25 loot items per run. Reaching pool exhaustion means you have pushed your hero further than most.

## Example Terminal Output

```
[ENCOUNTER SHIFT] You advance into [The Shattered Obsidian Citadel].
Lurking in the shadows emerges: [Malakar the Void-Eyed Lich]! What do you do?
```

<Info>
  Over a long run you may exhaust one or more individual pools while others still have entries remaining. This is intentional — each campaign has a finite universe of content, and depleting a pool is a milestone in its own right. The story continues with whatever pools remain until your HP reaches 0 or you choose to reset.
</Info>
